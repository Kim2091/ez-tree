# Dense canopy fitting

The **Fit Dense Canopy** button fits the current triangle budget using opaque mesh leaves. At larger budgets it also enables terminal sprigs; at tight budgets the extra stem geometry can reduce coverage. The budget thresholds are practical heuristics, not guarantees for every species or seed. Sprigs and blade detail can also be changed independently. Full remains the LOD0 default; LOD1 and LOD2 still default to Thin.

Sprigs add tapered geometry and leaf sites on the ends of deciduous branches. The fitter can remove whole sprigs before thinning individual leaves, so remaining blades stay attached to their supporting shoots. Willow retains foliage along its hanging shoots. Micro uses two-triangle oak blades and simplifies their lobed outline; ginkgo and sugar maple keep the more detailed Thin outlines needed for their characteristic shapes. Individual leaves and woody stems are real meshes with opaque materials, not alpha cards. The instanced GLB export currently instances leaf prototypes, not whole sprigs, and instancing does not reduce the number of triangles traced for each instance.

In a historical comparison at commit `861745a`, before the revised upward branch growth, the Oak Medium seed fitted at 25,000 triangles retained 2,147 sites with Thin and 6,441 with Micro, without sprigs. At 240,000 triangles Thin retained 16,424 sites; Micro with sprigs retained 64,264 sites at 233,720 triangles. These are site counts, not a claim of equivalent visual quality. An orthographic coverage proxy on that seed increased from 36.2% to 50.4% on one side view at 240k, comparing Micro without and with sprigs. Results vary by version, view, seed and species. Sprigs were less effective for several species at 25k, which is why the automatic fit omits them there. The [historical textured 240k comparison](dense-canopy-240k.png) is a preview render; a path-traced RTX Remix comparison remains necessary.

Research on [grid-based foliage simplification](https://nlpr.ia.ac.cn/2008papers/gjkw/gk23.pdf) and [cluster-based tree foliage](https://www.gametools.org/archives/publications/UJI_JCP2007.pdf) motivated retaining coverage across groups of leaves when reducing detail. [Real-Time GPU Tree Generation](https://gpuopen.com/download/Real-Time_GPU_Tree_Generation.pdf) describes varying individual leaf geometry with LOD. These papers do not validate the particular heuristic implemented here.
